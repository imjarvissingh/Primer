# Primer — Customer & Educator Validation Plan (public-safe)

Goal: replace hypotheses in [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md) and [YC-APPLICATION.md](YC-APPLICATION.md) with real evidence before and after the application. Nothing here has been done; no recruitment, outreach or data collection was run by the assistant.

## Ground rules

- **Adults only for now.** Interviews with parents/guardians and educators are conversations with adults. **No sessions with children** until the gates in [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md) are met: legal/COPPA review, verified parental consent, educator review of content, security/privacy review.
- Show adults only the synthetic demo; never ask them to enter their child's information. Tell them it is a draft with fake data.
- Record only what's needed. Keep notes **private, not in this public repo**; use initials or codes, not names. Ask permission before taking notes; don't record audio/video without consent.
- Report findings as what people said, with counts (e.g. "4 of 7 said…"). Don't call 5–10 interviews proof of demand.

## 1. Parent interviews (target 8–10, US, children 6–9)

Recruit from your own network and communities where parents of this age gather, asking for a 20-minute chat; avoid incentives that bias answers. Track: date, source, child age band, current math tools, key quotes (private log).

Questions (problem first, product last):
1. Tell me about the last time your child did math outside of school. What happened?
2. What do you use today (apps, workbooks, tutors, school tools)? What do you like/dislike?
3. How do you find out whether your child actually understands, versus got help? How much of your time does it take?
4. Have you paid for anything math-related? How much, how often, and why did you stop or continue?
5. What would make you trust a report on your child's progress? What would make you doubt it?
6. *(Show the demo, 2 minutes, synthetic.)* What do you think this is showing? What's unclear or missing?
7. What would you expect to pay, if anything, and for what? (Ask for past behavior, not hypothetical promises.)

Signals worth recording: unprompted pain, existing spend, workarounds, reaction to "independent vs. assisted" separation. Disconfirming evidence counts as much.

## 2. Elementary-math educator review (target 1–3)

Ask a qualified K–3 teacher or math specialist to review (not endorse the product): the skill list and prerequisites, the independent and practice items, hint wording, the draft (unapproved) mastery rules, theme vocabulary. Open questions are listed in [CURRICULUM.md](CURRICULUM.md) ("Other open educator questions"). Capture: what to change, what is wrong, what they would require before child use, and whether they would be paid or credited (agree this up front; don't imply endorsement in the application unless they agree in writing).

## 3. Competitor & alternatives scan (desk research)

List 8–12 existing products and non-product alternatives (workbooks, tutoring, school platforms). For each: audience, price, evidence claims, whether it separates assisted and unaided performance. Every claim needs a cited source and date. Output: a table plus one paragraph of honest differentiation.

## 4. Willingness-to-pay exploration

Use interview question 4 and 7 only. Don't present price lists. Note the difference between "would pay" and "have paid". Do not claim demand from compliments.

## 5. Evidence tracker (private)

Columns: ID, date, type (parent/educator/desk), source category, child age band, key finding, quote, supports/contradicts which hypothesis (H1/H2/H3 or problem/pricing), confidence, follow-up. Only the **aggregated, de-identified** result (counts and themes) goes into the application or this repo.

## 6. What this does not do

No pilot, no consent flow, no data collection from children, no outreach tool, no surveys with personal data. Those are separate workstreams behind the safety gates.

## Phase 8D addendum — logging discipline, educator criteria, falsification

(Additions only; sections 1–6 above remain the plan. Nothing here has been done.)

**Observed fact vs opinion.** In the private tracker keep two columns: _Observed_ (what the person did or said, or what happened: "paid $X last year for app Y", "could not tell which group to count") and _Interpretation_ (the founder's reading). Quote only what was said, never paraphrase into stronger claims. Mark each entry with who said it (parent/educator code), whether it is past behaviour or a hypothetical, and a confidence note. Public documents may contain only de-identified counts and themes.

**Recruiting educators (criteria).** Hold or have held a K–3 teaching credential, or work as an elementary math specialist/coach, with recent classroom experience at the 6–9 age band; ideally one reviewer with early-numeracy (subtraction/make-ten) experience and one with accessibility or special-education experience. No financial or personal relationship that would compromise candour. Agree up front whether the review is paid or credited; do not imply endorsement. Send them [EDUCATOR-REVIEW-PACKET.md](EDUCATOR-REVIEW-PACKET.md) and keep their completed record (section 8 of the packet) private unless they agree in writing to anonymised publication.

**Recruiting parents.** Own network and parent communities, one-to-one asks, no incentives that bias answers, no children present, demo shown only as synthetic. Do not contact anyone through automated tools.

**What would falsify the hypotheses (proposed; the founder should set the thresholds before interviewing — the "about a third" below is only a placeholder).**

- _Problem:_ fewer than about a third of interviewed parents describe a recent, unprompted difficulty knowing whether their child understands math (versus got help), or none have paid or tried anything to address it.
- _Theme (H1):_ educators or parents see no instructional value in interest-matched themes beyond decoration, or consider them a distraction.
- _Evidence (H3):_ parents do not distinguish independent from assisted evidence, or do not trust or act on a report of this kind.
- _Product validity:_ a qualified educator judges the make-ten sequence, hint disclosure or warm-up selection unsound and requires changes that remove the product's core flow.
- Treat a handful of interviews as signals, not proof; do not state demand from compliments.
